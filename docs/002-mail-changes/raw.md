**Swimming Galas – A Parent's Guide**

***What is a Swimming Gala?\***

*A swimming gala is simply the term used for a swimming competition. It  gives swimmers the opportunity to put into practice everything they have been working on in training whilst gaining valuable racing experience.*

*Galas range from small, friendly events aimed at beginners through to  National Championships featuring the country's top swimmers. Every  competition provides an opportunity for swimmers to develop confidence,  improve their skills and achieve personal best times.*

*Licensed competitions are run under* ***Swim England Rules and Regulations\*** *and are divided into four levels.*

***Level 1\*** *Long Course (50m) competitions used for National, Regional and County  Championships. These events allow swimmers to achieve qualifying times  for major championships.*

***Level 2\*** *Short Course (25m) competitions used for National, Regional and County  Championships. They provide qualifying opportunities for major  championships in a 25m pool.*

***Level 3\*** *Held in either 25m or 50m pools, these competitions allow swimmers to  achieve qualifying times for County and Regional Championships, as well  as higher-level meets.*

***Level 4\*** *Entry-level licensed competitions designed for developing swimmers. These events  provide an introduction to racing outside their own club and are an  excellent stepping stone towards Level 3 competitions.*

*Throughout the season, Torquay Leander swimmers regularly compete at Levels 1, 2  and 3, with newer swimmers beginning their competitive journey at Level 4 events.*

***Heats and Heat Declared Winners (HDW)\***

*Most swimming events have more competitors than there are lanes in the pool, so races are split into* ***heats\****. Each heat is simply a separate race within the same event.*

*Swimmers are normally seeded into heats based on their entry times, with the  slower swimmers competing in the earlier heats and the fastest swimmers  in the final heats. This helps ensure swimmers race against others of a  similar standard.*

*At many competitions, particularly Level 3 and Level 4 meets, the results are decided using* ***Heat Declared Winners (HDW)\****. This means there is* ***no final\****. Instead, every swimmer races once, and when all the heats have finished, the times are combined and ranked.*

*For example, if there are 40 swimmers entered into the 100m Freestyle, they may be divided into five heats of eight swimmers. A swimmer who wins  Heat 2 may not necessarily win the event if swimmers in later heats  record faster times. Once every heat has been completed, all times are  compared and medals are awarded to the fastest swimmers in each age  group.*

*At higher-level competitions, such as County, Regional and National Championships, some events may instead have* ***heats followed by finals\****. In these competitions, swimmers race in the heats first, and the  fastest qualifiers return later in the session (or day) to compete again in the final for medals and championship places.*

*Because of this, swimmers should always focus on swimming their own race and  achieving their best possible time, rather than simply trying to win  their individual heat.*

***Long Course and Short Course Galas\***

*Swimming competitions take place in two standard pool lengths:*

- **Short Course (SC)** – a **25-metre** pool.
- **Long Course (LC)** – a **50-metre** pool.

*When a club advertises a gala, it will state whether the competition is being held in a* ***Short Course\*** *or* ***Long Course\*** *pool.*

***Entry Times\***

*When entering a competition, swimmers are usually asked to provide an* ***entry time\*** *for each event. This is their best recorded time for the* ***full race distance\****, not just a single length of the pool.*

*For example:*

- A 100m Freestyle Short Course time is the swimmer's best **100m** time achieved in a **25m pool**.
- A 100m Freestyle Long Course time is the swimmer's best **100m** time achieved in a **50m pool**.

*Competition entry forms will normally specify whether they require* ***Short Course times\****,* ***Long Course times\****, or occasionally state that* ***all entry times should be 25m (Short Course) times\****.*

***Why Are the Times Different?\***

*Swimmers are often slightly quicker in a* ***25m pool\*** *because they complete more turns and push-offs from the wall, which provide extra speed.*

*As a result, Short Course and Long Course times are* ***not directly comparable\****, and competitions specify which type of time should be used for entries.*

***Time Conversions\***

*Some competitions request* ***Short Course times\*** *even though the event itself will be swum in a* ***Long Course pool\****. In these cases, organisers use the Short Course times to seed the heats.*

*For higher-level competitions, such as* ***Regional\*** *and* ***National Championships\****, swimmers are usually required to submit* ***Long Course qualifying times\****. If a swimmer has only achieved a qualifying time in a Short Course  pool, it may need to be converted using Swim England's approved  conversion system before entry.*

------

***Roles at a Gala\***

*Swimming competitions rely almost entirely on volunteers. Many of the people  you'll see around the pool are current or former swimming parents who  generously give their time to help the sport run smoothly.*

*If any of these roles interest you, we'd love to hear from you—we're always looking for new volunteers.*

***Coach\*** *Our coaches prepare swimmers before each race, oversee warm-ups and  cool-downs, and provide feedback afterwards. They help swimmers  understand their race plan and offer encouragement and technical advice  throughout the day.*

***Team Manager (TM)\****Team Managers are one of the most important roles at any gala.*

*They are trained volunteers who are responsible for the welfare, safety and  organisation of our swimmers throughout the competition. Every Team  Manager has completed Swim England training and holds an enhanced DBS  check.*

*Their responsibilities include:*

- *Looking after swimmers throughout the day.*
- *Acting in the swimmer's best interests, particularly when parents are not present.*
- *Making sure swimmers are ready for warm-up and races on time.*
- *Escorting swimmers to marshalling.*
- *Supporting the coaching team.*

*Without Team Managers, the club would be unable to attend competitions. We  always aim to have both male and female Team Managers available wherever possible.*

***Starter\*** *The Starter is responsible for ensuring each race begins fairly and safely by giving the official start.*

***Timekeepers\*** *Timekeepers record swimmers' finishing times. At many competitions electronic  timing equipment is used, with manual timekeepers providing an important backup.*

*The Head Timekeeper oversees the timing team and ensures all recorded times are accurate.*

***Judges and Referees\*** *Judges and Referees ensure every race is conducted fairly and according to Swim England rules.*

*Officials observe starts, turns, finishes and swimming technique. Any rule  infringements are reported to the Referee, who makes the final decision  regarding any disqualification.*

------

***What Should Swimmers Bring?\***

*Every gala is different, but we recommend bringing the following:*

- *Two swimsuits or racing costumes (if you have a separate race suit)*
- *Two pairs of goggles*
- *Two club swim hats*
- *Two towels*
- *Club T-shirt or hoodie*
- *Poolside footwear*
- *Plenty of water or sugar-free drinks*
- *Healthy snacks such as fruit, cereal bars, popcorn or rice cakes*
- *Lunch for full-day competitions (sandwiches, pasta, wraps etc.)*
- *A large bag to carry everything*
- *Mobile phone (if permitted by the venue, although many pools do not allow phones on poolside)*

*Any additional equipment required for a specific competition will be communicated before the event.*

------

***What Happens at a Gala?\***

*Although every competition is slightly different, the general routine is very similar.*

***Arrival\***

*Arrive at the venue in good time and report to one of our Team Managers. They  will direct swimmers to the changing rooms and let you know where the  team is based for the day.*

***Warm-Up\***

*Swimmers complete a supervised warm-up before racing. Boys' and girls' warm-ups  are usually held separately, and Team Managers or coaches will tell  swimmers when to report. The coach will explain exactly what swimmers  need to do during the warm-up.*

***Before Racing\***

*After warming up, swimmers should dry off, change into their racing costume  if required, and relax with the rest of the team. Many swimmers spend  this time chatting with friends, reading, playing cards or listening to  music while waiting to be called.*

***Marshalling\***

*When it's time for a race, the Team Manager will take swimmers to the coach  for a final briefing before escorting them to marshalling. Marshalling  is where swimmers are organised into heats before being taken to the  starting blocks.*

***The Race\*** *Once called forward, swimmers compete in their race.*

***After the Race\***

*After each swim, the coach will provide feedback, discussing what went well  and areas for improvement. Where facilities are available, swimmers may complete a warm-down swim before returning to the team area to dry off, refuel and prepare for their next event.*

***Going Home\***

*Once swimmers have completed all of their races, they must let their Team  Manager know before leaving with their parent or guardian.*

------

***Medals and Awards\***

*At most competitions there isn't enough time to hold formal medal  presentations during the event. Instead, medals and trophies are usually collected at the event. The main exception is our Club Championships  where we hold a designated awards night, usually in January where  trophies and medals are distributed and we celebrate the swimmers  acheivments!*

------

***A Final Word\***

*Above all, remember that swimming is meant to be enjoyable. Every gala is an  opportunity to learn, improve and represent Torquay Leander with pride.  Whether it's a swimmer's first competition or a National Championship,  we encourage every swimmer to support their teammates, enjoy the  experience and celebrate their own progress.*

*Personal bests, good sportsmanship and having fun are every bit as important as winning medals.*
